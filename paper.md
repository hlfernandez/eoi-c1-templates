# 🗣️ Oral Interaction

## Template

**[Introduction]**  
**A:** Hi B, how are you?  
**B:** Hi A, I’m fine, thanks. And you?  
**A:** Fine too! Today we are going to discuss three short texts about **[topic]**.  
First, we'll briefly comment on each one, giving our opinions, and then we’ll try to choose the one we find the most relevant. Would you like to start with one of the texts?

---

**[First Text]**  
**B:**  

- "Sure, I'll begin with the first text. It talks about **[brief idea of text 1]**."
- "Personally, I think it is **[interesting/important/limited]** because **[your reason]**."
- Optionally add:
  - "However, one possible drawback could be **[your reason]**."  
  - **OR** "But on the downside, **[mention a negative point]**."
- "What do you think?"

**A:** (Choose **one** reaction)

- "I see your point, and I also believe that **[build on their idea]**."
- "That's a good point. I hadn't thought about **[mention something they said]**."
- "I slightly disagree, because in my opinion **[your argument]**."
- "Exactly, and I would add that **[extra idea]**."
- "I see where you're coming from, but I see it a bit differently because **[your idea]**."

---

**[Second Text]**  
**A:**

- "Moving on to the second text, it focuses on **[brief idea of text 2]**."
- "In my view, it is **[relevant/less convincing/etc.]** because **[your reason]**."
- Optionally add:
  - "Nonetheless, it could be argued that **[counterpoint]**."
  - **OR** "Still, I think it misses **[something it lacks]**."
- "What's your opinion on this one?"

**B:** (React – again, choose **one** reaction.)

---

**[Third Text]**  
**B:**  

- "Finally, regarding the third text, it mentions **[brief idea of text 3]**."
- "I find it **[compelling/problematic]** because **[your reason]**."
- Optionally add:
  - "On the other hand, some people might argue that **[other perspective]**."
- "Would you agree?"

**A:** (React.)

---

**[Ending]**

✅ **If you reach an agreement:**  
**B/A:**  

- "So, it seems we both agree that the most relevant text is number **[X]**, because **[main reason]**."
- **OR** "We’re on the same page — text number **[X]** really hits the mark."

**A/B:** (Final brief comment — choose one)  

- "Yes, totally! I think we've made a good choice."  
- "Exactly, it covers the topic really well."  
- "I agree, it was quite clear from the beginning."

---

❌ **If you don’t reach an agreement:**  
**B/A:**  

- "Although we have different opinions, I think we’ve explored all three texts thoroughly."  
- **OR** "Seems like we see it a bit differently, but it’s been a good discussion."

**A/B:** (Final brief comment — choose one) 

- "Absolutely, it was interesting to hear your points."  
- "Yes, we had a good exchange of ideas."  
- "Definitely, it's good to see different perspectives."

## 🗣️ Example – Topic: Environment

---

**[Introduction]**  

**A:** Hi B, how are you?  
**B:** Hi A, I’m fine, thanks. And you?  
**A:** Fine too! Today we are going to discuss three short texts about **protecting the environment**.  
First, we'll briefly comment on each one, giving our opinions, and then we’ll try to choose the one we find the most relevant. Would you like to start with one of the texts?

---

**[First Text]**  
**B:**  

- "Sure, I'll begin with the first text. It talks about **making ecological awareness part of our culture and social habits**."
- "Personally, I think it’s **very important** because **education and awareness can lead to long-term changes in behaviour**."
- Optionally:  
  - "However, one possible drawback could be that **it takes a long time for cultural changes to happen**."
  - **OR** "But on the downside, **not everyone may be willing to change their habits just by learning about ecology**."
- "What do you think?"

**A:** (Choose one reaction)

- "I see your point, and I also believe that **education is key to motivating change**."
- "That's a good point. I hadn't thought about **how slow cultural change can be**."
- "I slightly disagree, because in my opinion **people often need stronger measures than just education**."
- "Exactly, and I would add that **talking to family and friends is a good way to spread small actions that can make a big difference**."
- "I see where you're coming from, but I see it a bit differently because **personal action needs to be backed by policies**."

---

**[Second Text]**  
**A:**  

- "Moving on to the second text, it focuses on **reducing car use by walking, cycling, or using electric vehicles**."
- "In my view, it is **extremely relevant** because **transportation is a major source of pollution**."
- Optionally:
  - "Nonetheless, it could be argued that **electric cars, although cleaner, still have an environmental impact during production**."
  - **OR** "Still, I think it misses **the point that in many cities, infrastructure for bikes or electric cars is still lacking**."
- "What's your opinion on this one?"

**B:** (React naturally, choosing one)

- "I agree, reducing emissions from cars would have an immediate impact."
- "Good point about the infrastructure — not all cities are ready for that change."
- "I see it differently because even small changes like walking short distances can already make a big difference."
- "Exactly, and I'd add that governments should encourage this by making cities more walkable."

---

**[Third Text]**  
**B:**  

- "Finally, regarding the third text, it mentions **minimizing the use of toxic chemicals to protect clean water sources**."
- "I find it **very compelling** because **access to clean water is a basic human right, and chemicals can do a lot of hidden damage**."
- Optionally:
  - "On the other hand, some people might argue that **industries are the main polluters and individual actions might not be enough**."
- "Would you agree?"

**A:** (React similarly.)

- "I completely agree — water is essential for life and we often take it for granted."
- "True, but I also think that without strong regulations, individual actions won't be enough."
- "I see your point, and I'd add that using eco-friendly products is an easy first step."

---

**[Ending]**

✅ **If you reach an agreement:**  
**A:**  

- "So, it seems we both agree that the most relevant text is number **three**, about protecting clean water, because **it's essential for survival and the impact is immediate**."

**B:**  

- "Yes, totally! Protecting water feels urgent and should be a priority."

---

❌ **If you don’t reach an agreement:**  
**A:**  

- "Although we have different opinions, I think we’ve explored all three texts thoroughly."

**B:**  

- "Absolutely, it was interesting to see the different aspects of environmental protection each text focuses on."

# 🗣️ Oral Mediation

## 🛠 TEMPLATE 1: Clear and Formal

**1. Introduction**  
_"Hi! I’ve recently read an article about **[topic]**, and I’d like to share the main points with you."_

**2. Main idea**  
_"The main idea of the article is that **[main idea in your own words]**."_

**3. Development / explanation**  
_"According to the article, **[summary of the situation/problem]**. It also mentions that **[example or additional point]**, and highlights that **[consequence or important fact]**."_

**4. Conclusion with appeal**  
_"That’s basically what the article says. I hope you find it as interesting as I did."_  

## 🛠 TEMPLATE 2: More Natural and Conversational

**1. Introduction**  
_"Hey! I just read an article about **[topic]**, and I thought you might be interested."_

**2. Main idea**  
_"The main message is that **[main idea in your own words]**."_

**3. Development / explanation**  
_"It explains that **[summary]**, and gives examples like **[example]**. It also points out that **[important consequence or key detail]**."_

**4. Conclusion with appeal**  
_"So that's basically it. I think you’d find it really relevant too."_

## 🏡 APPLICATION: Housing text using TEMPLATE 1

**1. Introduction**  
_"Hi! I’ve recently read an article about housing problems, and I’d like to share the main points with you."_

**2. Main idea**  
_"The main idea of the article is that governments are not doing enough to guarantee decent housing for everyone, and urgent action is needed."_

**3. Development / explanation**  
_"According to the article, around 1.8 billion people are currently living in very poor housing conditions. It also mentions that, while in poorer countries many people live in informal settlements, in richer countries there are increasing numbers of people living in tents, motels, shelters, or even cars. The article highlights that homelessness leads to preventable illnesses, endangers lives, and damages people's dignity, while governments often fail to address the problem effectively."_

**4. Conclusion with appeal**  
_"That’s basically what the article says. I hope you find it as interesting as I did."_

## Checklist

Here's a **short, practical checklist** you can use during the **2 minutes of preparation** before you speak:  
It's designed to be **very quick to fill in**, **very easy to follow** when you talk.

### 1. Introduction  
- Topic: **[housing, law, health, etc.]**  
_(Just a word or two to remember.)_

### 2. Main Idea  
- Main point: **[What is the article trying to say overall? One sentence!]**

### 3. Key Information  
- Problem/situation: **[What is happening?]**  
- Example/s: **[One or two examples mentioned.]**  
- Consequence/s: **[Effect or importance according to the article.]**

### 4. Conclusion / Appeal  
- Closing phrase:  
  - _"Hope you find it interesting too."_  
  - or _"Thought it might be relevant for you as well."_

# 🗣️ Oral Production

## 🛠 TEMPLATE

**1. Introduction (15–20 seconds)**  
_"Today, I’d like to share some thoughts about **[topic]**. It’s a subject that raises important questions about **[short general comment on the topic]**."_

Useful connectors:

- "To begin with,"
- "First of all,"

**2. Opinion and Analysis (1–1.5 minutes)**  
_"In my opinion, **[your first point]**. This is because **[reason]**."_

_"Another aspect to consider is **[second point]**, since **[reason or example]**."_

_"Moreover, it’s important to highlight that **[third point if needed]**."_

Useful connectors:

- "I believe that..."
- "One clear effect is that..."
- "Another important factor is..."
- "This leads to..."

**3. Predictions and Speculation (30–40 seconds)**  
_"Looking into the future, I think that **[prediction about how things might change]**."_

_"It is possible that **[speculative idea]**, especially if **[condition]**."_

Useful connectors:

- "In the future,"
- "It is likely that..."
- "We might see..."
- "There is a chance that..."

**4. Conclusion / Ending (10–15 seconds)**  
_"To sum up, **[summary of your main ideas]**. It’s clear that **[final general comment]**."_

Useful connectors:

- "In conclusion,"
- "All in all,"
- "To wrap up,"

# Oral Mediation and Production

## Example: "Hidden Identity"

### Task Statement

**TOPIC: IDENTITY**  
**HIDDEN IDENTITY**

You read the following text in a news article:

> We have two identities: one visible and other hidden. There are things practically impossible to hide, however, we also have characteristics that we do not want to reveal. These characteristics make up our hidden IDENTITY.
> 
> There are many reasons that lead us to want to hide some aspects of our identity. We can think, for example, that those who make up our social network will reject us if they know the truth, for example, or maybe we just want to avoid conflicts because we know they think differently. Perhaps we feel obliged to hide certain aspects of our identity because they represent a social stigma, or simply because we want to continue enjoying certain privileges that would be prohibited to our authentic identity.

You are at a conference about psychology trends with your partner and decide to inform them about the contents of the previous text and give your opinion about it.

**TASK ONE: ORAL MEDIATION**

Speaking time: 1–2 minutes. Talk about these points:

- Explain what you have read in your own words.
- Identify the main idea of the article.

**TASK TWO: PRODUCTION**

Speaking time: 2–3 minutes. Talk about the following points and support your opinions with arguments:

- Discuss the effects of hiding your personality.
- Make predictions and speculate about what the situation may be like in the future.

### Task One: Oral Mediation (1–2 minutes)

Hi! I recently read an article about hidden aspects of personal identity, and I would like to share the main points with you.

The main idea of the article is that every person has both a visible and a hidden identity, and many people choose to conceal certain aspects of themselves for different reasons.

The article explains that while some personal traits are impossible to hide, others are intentionally kept secret. The reasons for hiding them can vary: fear of rejection by social groups, the desire to avoid conflicts due to differences in opinions, or the wish to prevent social stigma. In some cases, people may also want to maintain certain privileges they would lose if their full identity were known.

That is basically what the article says. I think you would find it quite interesting too.

### Task Two: Oral Production (2–3 minutes)

Today, I would like to share some thoughts about hidden identity. It is a subject that raises important questions about how we present ourselves to society and the consequences of concealing aspects of who we are.

To begin with, hiding parts of our personality can have strong effects on our mental health. I believe that pretending to be someone else, even partially, can cause anxiety and stress. People might constantly feel fear of being discovered.  
Another important effect is that relationships might become superficial. If we are not being authentic, it is harder to form real, deep connections with others. Moreover, society as a whole can suffer when individuals feel pressured to hide due to discrimination or prejudice.

Looking into the future, I think that people may feel increasingly free to express their true identities. With growing awareness around diversity and inclusion, it is likely that social acceptance will improve. However, there might still be resistance in certain environments, especially where traditional norms are deeply rooted.

To sum up, hiding aspects of identity can have serious personal and social consequences, but the situation may evolve positively in the future if attitudes continue to change.
